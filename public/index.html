    How StoryWeaver works

    An agent crew that weaves stories you can step into.

    StoryWeaver is a prototype of an immersive storytelling agent. Below: what we mean by immersive, the agents that make it happen, how a story flows through them, and the design decisions — and trade-offs — behind it.

    What "immersive" means here

    Immersion is the moment you stop noticing the app and start living the story. Five things get you there:

    🎧
    Hear itA narrator and a distinct voice for every character, music that rises with the tension, and sounds that belong to the scene.
    🖼️
    See itA painted picture for every minute of story, appearing at its most striking moment, with slow camera moves and weather in the scene.
    📖
    Follow itClassic storytelling: the world and hero first, then an adventure where every event follows from the last.
    🧭
    Shape itEvery story has a moment where you choose, or just say what you'd like ("add a dragon!") — the story listens, adapts, and the newcomer joins the cast.
    ⏱️
    Fit itSized to the time you have, landing its ending on time — and the hero can carry your name.

    Meet the crew

    Ten roles, each with one job. Only two call a language model for each story; plain code and your browser do everything that should be instant and predictable. The curtain rises on them one by one — tap anyone in the line-up to bring them back into the spotlight.

    How a story flows

    One LangGraph workflow on a stateless Python API: the Guardian and the Storyteller work in parallel, the Editor checks every line, and the whole story — both endings included — reaches your browser before it begins. While you listen, nothing waits.

    Safety in four layers

    Children may be listening, so safety is designed in — not bolted on. Deterministic rules come first because they are instant and can't be argued with; models handle nuance.
